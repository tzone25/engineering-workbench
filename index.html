Hydraulic Fault-Finding Reference

No pressure

Check reservoir level, obvious leaks, pump rotation/drive condition, suction condition, relief valve condition, valve position and machine-specific controls. Electrical testing and controls work must be performed by an appropriately qualified person.

Low pressure

Consider relief valve setting, pump wear, internal leakage, actuator leakage, excessive system demand and pressure losses.

Slow actuator

Compare measured flow against expected flow. Consider pump displacement, engine/motor speed, restriction, valve condition and leakage.

High temperature

Look for excessive pressure drop, relief flow, internal leakage, restricted cooler airflow/flow, incorrect oil viscosity and contamination.

Cavitation

Typical clues include noise, vibration, unstable operation and damaged pump components. Check suction restriction, reservoir level and inlet conditions.

Case drain high

Compare case-drain flow against the manufacturer's allowable value. Increasing case drain can indicate internal pump or motor wear.

Contamination

Check filtration, breather condition, seals, reservoir cleanliness, oil condition and maintenance practices.

Air ingress

Inspect suction hoses, clamps, seals and reservoir level. Foaming and erratic movement can be clues.

Internal leakage

Compare pressure, temperature and flow measurements with manufacturer limits before condemning a component.

Mechanical Engineering Reference

Bearings

Record bearing number, shaft diameter, housing, fit, lubrication, temperature, speed, load and operating history.

Shafts

Consider torque, bending, torsion, stress concentration, keyways, fatigue, surface condition and material.

Fits

Common fit concepts include clearance, transition and interference fits. Select according to load, rotation, temperature and assembly method.

Keys & Keyways

Check key size, fit, seating, shaft/keyway condition, fretting and torque transmission requirements.

Fasteners

Record size, grade, thread, lubrication condition and specified tightening method. Use manufacturer/site torque requirements where applicable.

Seals

Consider shaft speed, pressure, temperature, fluid compatibility, surface finish and installation condition.

Gears

Check ratio, module/DP, tooth condition, backlash, alignment, lubrication and load.

Chains

Check pitch, sprocket condition, alignment, lubrication, tension and elongation.

Lubrication

Record lubricant type, quantity, interval, contamination and temperature. Never mix lubricants unless compatibility is confirmed.

Engineering Formula Reference

Power

P = T × ω. For rotational systems: P(kW) = T(Nm) × RPM / 9550.

Stress

σ = F/A. Ensure units are consistent before interpreting results.

Torque

T = F × r.

Flow

Q = displacement × rotational speed × efficiency.

Pressure

Pressure = Force / Area.

Thermal expansion

ΔL = αLΔT.

Engineering Reference Library

Hydraulics

Pressure, flow, cylinder sizing, pump displacement, hydraulic power, contamination, cavitation, internal leakage and temperature diagnostics.

Bearings

Record bearing number, fit, lubrication, speed, load, temperature and condition. Always check OEM limits.

Materials

Steel, stainless steel, aluminium, brass, bronze and engineering plastics require different design and machining considerations.

GD&T

Reference concepts include datum, position, flatness, straightness, parallelism, perpendicularity, concentricity and runout.

Tolerances

Record nominal size, upper limit, lower limit, actual measurement and inspection equipment used.

Failure Modes

Wear, fatigue, overload, corrosion, contamination, misalignment, poor lubrication, thermal damage and installation error.

Root Cause

Separate immediate cause, contributing cause and underlying/systemic cause.

5 Whys

Repeatedly ask why until the controllable underlying cause is identified and corrective action can be assigned.

WHS

Identify hazards, control risks, verify isolation, control stored energy and follow site procedures before work.
